# Slay the Spire: why players love it, and what Temperance Lanes can take from it

Research done on 26 November 2026 for Temperance Lanes. It covers Slay the Spire (2019) and, where useful, Slay the Spire 2 (Early Access, March 2026).

**About the sources.** Reddit could not be reached directly or through mirrors from this session, so Reddit voices appear only second-hand (one thread reported by GamesRadar). Player voices come from Steam discussion threads, two pages of top-rated Steam reviews (ten reviews each, so the counts below are a rough guide only), the Quarter to Three, Giant Bomb and Sirlin forums, a MetaFilter thread, player essays (Jorbs, Zvi Mowshowitz, ForgottenArbiter) and critics. Designer views come from interviews and from third-party summaries of the GDC 2019 talk; the talk itself was not watched, so details from it are second-hand. Some design-analysis pages (saschb2b's game-mechanics notes) are secondary and partly unsourced; they are used for framing, not as evidence.

## 1. What players say they love

### Many small decisions that each matter (comes up most, and most strongly)
The most common praise is that nearly every screen is a real choice. Zvi Mowshowitz describes the game as "lots of choices, each a randomized multiple choice": three card picks, shop stock, which card to upgrade, which event option ([LessWrong review](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MEimkSwwuRgiPaZaY/review-slay-the-spire)). He stresses how little rule complexity sits underneath: five cards, three energy, complexity added a piece at a time. In the ten top-rated Steam reviews, four praise the strategy behind which cards you take ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&l=english)). Anthony Giovannetti, the designer, says he layered map routing on top of card play on purpose, because layered decisions add depth "without much complexity" ([Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-mega-crit-games-i-slay-the-spire-i-)). He also chose an energy limit so hands are played thoughtfully rather than dumped ([PC Gamer interview](https://pcgamer.com/slay-the-spire-designer-discusses-new-characters-and-card-game-inspirations)).

### A build coming together, often from an odd start (common, strong)
Players love making a deck that is theirs. The AV Club piece credits the game's "encouragement of creativity" and relics that reshape how you play ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/why-slay-the-spire-has-such-staying-power)). A Sirlin forum player won at Ascension 20 by building around Searing Blow, a card they call usually "one of the most useless" in the game ([Sirlin forums](https://forums.sirlingames.com/t/slay-the-spire/8311)). Giovannetti names the feeling of a deck "coming together" as a main design goal, and says he wanted players pushed "to adapt on the fly" rather than repeat one deck ([Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-mega-crit-games-i-slay-the-spire-i-); [PC Gamer interview](https://pcgamer.com/slay-the-spire-designer-discusses-new-characters-and-card-game-inspirations)). The test is not reaching the end but arriving strong enough: PC Gamer's line is that "the hard part is being a god when you arrive" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/part-of-slay-the-spires-brilliance-is-that-getting-to-the-end-isnt-the-hard-part/)).

### Hard, but losses feel earned (common; disputed by a vocal minority)
AV Club says losses rarely feel like cheating, and a good run is usually only a few tries away ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/why-slay-the-spire-has-such-staying-power)). PC Gamer calls deaths from chasing elites "calculated deaths": you chose the risk ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/part-of-slay-the-spires-brilliance-is-that-getting-to-the-end-isnt-the-hard-part/)). In Slay the Spire 2's reviews, one player argues people blame balance for what was bad luck or "an accumulation of many small mistakes" ([Steam reviews, StS2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2868840/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&l=english)). The counter-view is covered in section 4.

### Skill shows over many runs (common among experienced players)
The strongest evidence that choices matter is that top players win most runs at the highest difficulty. ForgottenArbiter reports TerrenceM at roughly 40-72% per character at Ascension 20 including the final Heart fight, and Lifecoach at 94% on Watcher; no unwinnable seed has been found in several billion searched ([Is every seed winnable?](https://forgottenarbiter.github.io/Is-Every-Seed-Winnable)). Jorbs, a well-known streamer, reported 15-35% at the time he wrote, and frames each floor as a new test rather than the deck as a finished thing ([Jorbs on Medium](https://medium.com/@JoINrbs/solving-the-spire-better-living-through-card-games-1e0dae4040aa)). That gap between strong players and everyone else is what players mean by depth.

### Fun even when losing (common)
A Reddit player found 7 wins against 637 losses after 300 hours and kept playing; replies said much the same ([GamesRadar on the Reddit thread](https://www.gamesradar.com/roguelike-fan-appalled-to-discover-that-after-spending-300-hours-on-one-game-theyve-only-managed-7-victories-this-is-brutal)). AV Club notes that doomed runs still offer builds and discoveries along the way ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/why-slay-the-spire-has-such-staying-power)).

## 2. The mechanics behind those feelings

### Enemy intents: the threat is shown before you act
Each enemy shows what it will do next turn. Zvi calls this the game's "central innovation": it turns damage into a planning problem ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/MEimkSwwuRgiPaZaY/review-slay-the-spire)). The intent system was not in the original design; it came out of hundreds of hours of playtesting, per an Ars Technica interview summarised by [Nintygamer](https://nintygamer.com/slay-the-spire-war-stories/). One boss relic, Runic Dome, gives an extra energy each turn in exchange for hiding intents ([dood.gg relic guide](https://dood.gg/en/slay-the-spire/guides/relic-guide)), which shows how much the information is worth: it is a price you can pay.

### Small offers, and "take nothing" is a real answer
Card rewards are three cards, and you can skip. Players treat skipping as a skill: a Quarter to Three regular warns that taking too many generic cards is "an excellent way to lose" ([QT3 forum](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/slay-the-spire/134927?page=12)), and new-player threads advise passing on weak cards while waiting for one to build around ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1761356057430841314)). Skipping matters because every card you add dilutes the ones you want to draw. Even on Steam, players argue about whether a middling card beats skipping ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/3883851089741526718)).

### Deck thinning at a rising price
Shops remove a card for gold, and the price rises each time; Hipsters of the Coast thinks this is fairly balanced but that slim decks still dominate ([Hipsters of the Coast](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2022/03/slay-the-spire)). Advice to remove Strikes whenever you can is standard, though experienced players note top streamers often run 25-30 cards, so thin is not the only way ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1761356057430841314)).

### Map pathing as chosen risk
Each act is a branching map of fights, elites, shops, rest sites, treasure and "?" events. Elites always give a relic but cost health; rest sites heal or upgrade a card, not both ([saschb2b map notes](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/slay-the-spire/map-and-nodes)). Giovannetti took path choice from FTL ([Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-mega-crit-games-i-slay-the-spire-i-)). Forum players talk in budgets: one plans for 25-50% of health lost per elite ([QT3](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/slay-the-spire/134927?page=12)). A study of 20,000 Ironclad runs found that winning runs took more uncertain paths, though the link is correlational ([Bazzaz and Cooper, FDG 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03918v1)). Hipsters of the Coast likes that you "chart your own course" ([Hipsters](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2022/03/slay-the-spire)).

### Known bosses, and a mid-game check
Each act's boss has a portrait at the top of the map ([wiki: Bosses](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Bosses)), so you can draft and route toward it (my reading is that it shows from the start of the act; the wiki does not say when). The final boss is only revealed when Act 3 begins, which a Giant Bomb poster calls impossible to plan for (see section 4). AV Club names a "death zone" from the first boss to mid-Act 2 that shows whether a deck is viable ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/why-slay-the-spire-has-such-staying-power)).

### Boss relics: big power with a cost attached
After an act boss you pick one of three boss relics. The energy ones each carry a drawback: Coffee Dripper (no resting), Ectoplasm (no more gold), Sozu (no potions), Busted Crown (one card choice instead of three), Runic Dome (no intents), Velvet Choker (six cards a turn) ([dood.gg relic guide](https://dood.gg/en/slay-the-spire/guides/relic-guide)). AV Club singles these out as making you think about your play in the abstract ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/why-slay-the-spire-has-such-staying-power)). Slay the Spire 2 replaces boss relics with "Ancients" met at the start of each act, whose boons Mega Crit says come with significant trade-offs ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/ancients)); GamesRadar's review says this makes choices more about the deck ([GamesRadar review](https://www.gamesradar.com/slay-the-spire-2-review/)).

### Events you cannot drift through
For Slay the Spire 2, Mega Crit reworked events to avoid ones where you "simply do nothing and pass through" ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/events-redesign)).

### Ascension: one new constraint per level
Twenty levels, each adding one rule on top of the last: more elites, harder enemies, less healing, a curse in the starting deck, one fewer potion slot, two final bosses at level 20 ([wiki: Ascension](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Ascension)). Players say the base game is easy once understood and that Ascension is where the game lives ([Giant Bomb forum](https://www.giantbomb.com/slay-the-spire/3030-59383/forums/my-frustrations-with-slay-the-spire-100-hours-late-1822259/?page=1); [QT3](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/slay-the-spire/134927?page=12)). Mega Crit also used the levels to tune for different skill tiers ([BriefRead summary of the GDC talk](https://briefread.pages.dev/en-GB/summary/slay-the-spire-metrics-driven-design)).

### Balancing by pick rate and win-deck presence
Mega Crit logged every choice from the prototype on. Giovannetti says the two most useful numbers are how often a card is picked when offered and how often it appears in winning decks ([Game Developer, 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-devs-use-data-to-balance-their-roguelike-deck-builder)). The aim was not equal power but that every card has a job, as a build-around or a staple ([BriefRead summary](https://briefread.pages.dev/en-GB/summary/slay-the-spire-metrics-driven-design)). Raw numbers can mislead: Madness looked overpowered only because players took it late in runs that were already winning (same source).

## 3. Breaking the game

### What it looks like
Infinite combos exist. A well-known early one stripped the deck to two cards, Flash of Steel and Dual Wield, which loop until the enemy dies; Kotaku found it delightful and noted it took "so much effort and luck" to reach, mainly removing every starting card ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/slay-the-spire-has-some-fun-infinite-combos-1822692745)). Strong non-infinite builds also break bosses: the Searing Blow player sat back through the Awakened One's second phase "until I could one-shot him" ([Sirlin forums](https://forums.sirlingames.com/t/slay-the-spire/8311)).

### How players feel
Mostly positively, when it was hard to get there. Kotaku praised the ways players "break the game" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/slay-the-spire-has-some-fun-infinite-combos-1822692745)); a QT3 player likes that "not all the crazy edges have been sanded off" ([QT3](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/slay-the-spire/134927?page=12)). The complaint runs the other way in Slay the Spire 2: PC Gamer's Evan Lahti finds most combos simple multiplication and the game too conservative ([PC Gamer, 12 opinions](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/slay-the-spire-2-opinions/)). In a Steam thread, a player who has never gone infinite asks how; others say it is not easy in practice, and that recent patches ended an easy way to cheese Ascension 10 ([Steam thread, StS2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2868840/discussions/0/798964766336027485/)). How widely players miss the nerfed infinites is uncertain; the coverage found reflects individual writers more than a measured reaction.

### How the design keeps it earned
- **Rare, hard to assemble, needs searching.** The GDC talk's stated rule: strong combinations are fine in a single-player game if they are rare, hard to assemble, or need active searching. Its example is Corruption plus Dead Branch, which you may never complete, or may skip the first half of before seeing the second ([Glasp summary of the GDC talk](https://glasp.co/youtube/7rqfbvnO_H0)).
- **Infinites should be rare** because they make play trivial; Dual Wield was buffed, then let players go infinite, then was cut back to copying only certain cards ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-devs-use-data-to-balance-their-roguelike-deck-builder)).
- **Harder, not removed.** Slay the Spire 2's first big patch nerfed a long list of loop cards; Mega Crit called infinites "a fundamental part of the StS 2 power curve" and wants them harder to reach ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/patch-notes-infinites-nerfed)).
- **The path costs something.** A two-card deck has given up block, scaling and answers; thinning costs gold and shop visits ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/slay-the-spire-has-some-fun-infinite-combos-1822692745)). Some bosses tax specific engines: the Awakened One punishes heavy use of Power cards and Time Eater punishes card-spam decks ([Giant Bomb forum](https://www.giantbomb.com/slay-the-spire/3030-59383/forums/my-frustrations-with-slay-the-spire-100-hours-late-1822259/?page=1)).

## 4. Why losses feel fair, and where they don't

### What makes them feel fair
- **The threat is visible.** Intents and learnable enemy patterns. In a 65-reply Steam thread asking to "dial back the RNG about 90%", most defenders point to learnable patterns and say losses reflect decisions ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1620600279681610030)).
- **You chose the risk.** Elite paths, greedy relic picks, skipped rest sites: the death traces back to a decision ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/part-of-slay-the-spires-brilliance-is-that-getting-to-the-end-isnt-the-hard-part/)).
- **Top-player results.** High win rates at the hardest level show that choices outweigh luck over many runs ([ForgottenArbiter](https://forgottenarbiter.github.io/Is-Every-Seed-Winnable)).
- **Short distance back.** A good run is a few tries away ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/why-slay-the-spire-has-such-staying-power)).

### Common criticisms
- **No build-around turns up.** The Giant Bomb thread's main complaint: in weak runs the key piece arrives too late or never, and you draft "aimlessly" ([Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/slay-the-spire/3030-59383/forums/my-frustrations-with-slay-the-spire-100-hours-late-1822259/?page=1)). Hipsters of the Coast likes the learning curve but says variance can block skilled players ([Hipsters](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2022/03/slay-the-spire)).
- **Hard counters you could not see coming.** The random final boss, revealed only in Act 3, punishes some decks; the same poster has never beaten the Awakened One with certain builds ([Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/slay-the-spire/3030-59383/forums/my-frustrations-with-slay-the-spire-100-hours-late-1822259/?page=1)). In Slay the Spire 2, the boss Aeonglass draws the same charge: it fills your deck with status cards and punishes several strategies, "King Punish II" ([PC Gamer, 12 opinions](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/slay-the-spire-2-opinions/)). This is the clearest line between fair and unfair: a test you could prepare for versus one that deletes a plan you could not have known to avoid.
- **Spiky encounters and inconsistent damage** ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1620600279681610030)).
- **Early game that is punishing but not a decision.** PC Gamer's Austin Wood calls Slay the Spire 2's Act 1 "too punishing and too boring", with pathing and drafting often not mattering ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/slay-the-spire-2-opinions/)). Several Slay the Spire 2 Steam reviews complain about bosses and elites (six of ten in a small sample) ([Steam reviews, StS2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2868840/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&l=english)).
- **Too easy without Ascension; lucky runs play themselves.** One QT3 player says some runs are so lucky "you couldn't even bad-decision your way into a loss" ([QT3](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/slay-the-spire/134927?page=12)).
- **One dominant style.** Slim decks dominate ([Hipsters](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2022/03/slay-the-spire)); in Slay the Spire 2 one critic says archetypes "are often a lie" next to one strong card ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/card-games/slay-the-spire-2-opinions/)).

## 5. Implications for Temperance Lanes

Ordered by value for effort. Each names the finding it comes from and the current issue it addresses: **one engine or lose at game 7**, **repeatable opener**, **unclear shop value**, **more say over the board without full control (knock or wait plans)**, **fair losses**. Examples below are untested sketches, not things that exist in the game. Per the project rule, each should be settled by building something to play.

1. **Track pick rate and win-run presence for every piece.** *Finding:* Mega Crit's two key numbers, read with context (the Madness trap). *Addresses:* unclear shop value, and the dead-pieces item in `docs/IDEATION.md`. The Results log already records offers and buys; a bot or log pass that reports, per piece, "offered, bought, in winning runs" would show which pieces players skip because they are weak and which because their value is unreadable. Cheapest item here.

2. **Show the board's next ball, like an intent.** *Finding:* intents are the most-cited source of fairness and planning, and Runic Dome shows the information is worth a full energy. *Addresses:* fair losses; say over the board. Some exists already (the front-row warning, break-through cost shown on the lane). The step further is a readable preview, before each throw, of where each standing pin will be and what it will be worth after this ball, and which would cross the red line, so a loss to a break-through always traces to a ball you chose to leave. Whether it needs to be always on or on a hold is a feel question to build and play.

3. **Make every offer show what it changes, and make "not buying" a real choice.** *Finding:* players value skipping because adding a card has a cost (dilution); offers are small and comparable. *Addresses:* unclear shop value; one engine or lose. In Temperance Lanes, buying a pin or power displaces something, so the cost exists but is hidden (the IDEATION note on level-up versus replace). Show the before/after on the slot, and consider a reason to bank cash. A rising-price removal service (clear a power off a pin) is the direct counterpart of Slay the Spire's card removal and would reward focusing powers on a few pins, which Josh's spread-out runs suggest the game does not yet do.

4. **A light route choice between games.** *Finding:* map pathing turns fixed fights into chosen risks, elites trade health for relics, and winners took riskier paths. *Addresses:* one engine or lose (a way to buy power early by taking risk); repeatable opener (games differ by choice); knock or wait plans (routes can favour one). A sketch: after a shop, pick the next game from two, one harder with a guaranteed strong piece, one easier with a smaller purse. The Five-Pin Variety Lab's paid "next rack" choice is a precedent. Uncertain: two choices may be enough; Slay the Spire's full map may be too much screen for a phone with no scrolling.

5. **Bosses should tax a plan, not delete it, and the counter should be buyable in time.** *Finding:* Slay the Spire's fairest tests are announced in advance (act boss on the map); its most criticised are hard counters revealed too late (random final boss, Aeonglass). *Addresses:* fair losses; one engine or lose. Temperance Lanes already names the level's boss ahead, and the 24 November tuning softened bosses that locked builds out (Flat rate, Cold lane). Next: make sure at least one answer to the coming boss can appear in the shops before it, and check with bots that every plan can beat every boss with preparation.

6. **Big pieces with a price after each boss.** *Finding:* boss relics and Slay the Spire 2's Ancients give run-defining power with a drawback, forcing commitment. *Addresses:* one engine or lose (a planned moment to commit to an engine before the game-7 jump rather than drifting); knock or wait plans (the cost can lean a run one way). A sketch: one of three pieces, each strong for knocking or for waiting and each closing off something. Keep the count small; Slay the Spire offers three.

7. **Let engines break the game late, from two or more pieces found separately.** *Finding:* players love breaking it when it was hard to assemble; Mega Crit's rule is rare, hard to assemble, needs searching, and Slay the Spire 2 made infinites harder rather than removing them. *Addresses:* one engine or lose. The current multiplier cap of 32 is a hard ceiling; Slay the Spire's answer is a long road plus bosses that tax specific engines. Worth testing whether a looser ceiling reached only by a two-piece combo (as Corruption plus Dead Branch) feels better than a fixed cap. Uncertain: arcade scoring may make "broken" mean little unless targets keep pace.

8. **Vary what each game asks, not the starting rack.** *Finding:* Giovannetti wanted players forced to adapt, with enemies that test different strategies. *Addresses:* repeatable opener. The opening rack stays fixed by Josh's choice. The Slay the Spire parallel is that the deck is the same but each fight asks something different. Small and big games could carry one visible rule each (shown in advance, like an intent) so the best first ball changes. Uncertain: this overlaps with item 4 and may be the same feature.

9. **Treat the game-7 jump as a death zone players can see coming.** *Finding:* players accept Slay the Spire's mid-game check because it is visible and you can prepare. *Addresses:* one engine or lose; fair losses. The jump itself may be fine; what is missing is levers to prepare (items 3 to 6) and a clear signal. Showing the target ladder ahead already helps.

10. **Later: a difficulty staircase, one constraint per step.** *Finding:* Ascension is where experienced players say the game lives. *Addresses:* long-term depth more than any current issue; the progress system already covers early unlocks.
