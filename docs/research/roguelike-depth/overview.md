## What the three reports agree on

These three reports cover Balatro, Slay the Spire, and games that mix a skill shot or physics with building (Peglin, Ballionaire, Ultrapool, Ball x Pit, Luck be a Landlord, Dicey Dungeons). Between them they draw on about 85 sources. Reddit couldn't be fetched directly, so what Reddit players think comes second-hand, through other sites.

### 1. Needing a scoring engine is fine. The problem is not being able to reach one.

Balatro players accept that late games need an engine. They complain when the pieces don't show up, or when only one build is worth doing. For Temperance Lanes, that means giving both plans, knocking pins down and waiting to harvest, their own route to an engine by level 2. It also means ways to switch plans: selling a power back, moving it to another pin, or removing it for a fee that rises each time. That lets a half-built run change direction before game 7 instead of dying there.

*Addresses: you need one engine or you lose around game 7; both plans should work.*

### 2. A loss feels fair when the threat was shown before you chose.

Chance feels fair when it lands before a decision and unfair when it lands after one. A ball's scatter is the unfair kind, so fairness has to come from what you know before you throw. That means showing the boss while you can still shop, making sure an answer to it can turn up in the shops before it, and having bosses weaken a plan rather than shut it out.

*Addresses: lost runs should feel fair.*

### 3. Show more of the board.

Slay the Spire's most-praised feature is showing what the enemy will do next turn. The Temperance Lanes version would be a preview, before each throw, of where each standing pin will be next ball, what it will be worth, and which pins will cross the red line. Balatro adds a tally that adds up step by step after each ball, so you can tell a weak build from a bad bounce.

*Addresses: more say over the board without full control; fair losses.*

### 4. Break up the repeatable opener by changing what each game asks, not the rack.

Each game could come with one condition, shown before it starts, so the best first ball changes from game to game. Slay the Spire's map, where you pick a harder or easier next fight, is a related idea.

*Addresses: the repeatable opener.*

### 5. Breaking the game is welcome if it comes late and takes work.

Players love finding something overpowered when it was hard to put together. Caps could be lifted only in level 4 or in an endless mode after a win, and reaching them could take two pieces found separately. Strong pieces are better held back by price and rarity than by hard caps. When a combo is weakened, say so in the game.

*Addresses: gives each plan a big payoff to aim for.*

## What I'd build first

- **Per-piece numbers in the log:** how often each piece is offered, how often it's bought, and how often it's in a winning run. This is the cheapest step, and it would show which pieces are weak and which are only hard to judge.
- **A Pieces Lab test** of the next-ball preview and a sell or move option for powers.

Each report ends with its own ranked list of changes, with the reasons and sources behind each one.
