Five blind pitches

Five designers each got the same brief: the Ten-Pin verdict, the six reasons the loop collapses, and your notes. None saw the others. A critic then scored them and measured, in the sim, what a player can actually aim at. Pick two or three to play as small labs, or mix parts.

Wildcard · critic #1

Alarm-Pin Heist

The rack stays for four balls. Gold-collared pins pay; any ball that drops a red alarm pin pays nothing.

Before each ball: Take loot now, or spend a $0 ball to knock out an alarm and open the pocket. Once the quota is met: walk away with the spare balls, or keep throwing and risk the surplus.

Lab: Easiest of the five: pin collars, a quota bar, a WALK button, a rack that doesn’t reset.

Risk: Alarms away from the headpin need exact pins kept standing, which the sim says is about 1 unit wide. Its worked example’s “thin the headpin” line doesn’t exist.

Systems designer · critic #2

Holdover Rack

Pins still standing at a frame’s end stay put and gain value; only the gaps refill, from a bag of pins you build.

Before each ball: Cash a ripe pin now or let it grow. Some pins pay only to a direct ball hit, so the strike can be the worst throw.

Lab: Easy: a rule change plus a Pass button.

Risk: Its key play, leaving the 7-10 on purpose, is 1.5 px wide. Ripening has to ask for a side or one pin, never a named leave.

Bowling designer · critic #3

Lane Breakdown

Every ball dries the boards it rolls on, and the oil lasts a whole level, so your strike line drifts and dies.

Before each ball: Chase the worn track, open a fresh one, or throw a plastic spare ball that burns nothing. The boss game is bowled on the lane you leave.

Lab: Easy: about 20 lines to push the ball along its hook, plus drawn tracks.

Risk: Your usual line has 7 units of slack on one side; parking the ring there gives about 8 strikes before you must move, so chasing may become arithmetic.

Art director and worldbuilder · critic #4

Hold the Lane

Your invaders idea: the board never resets. Walkers step toward you each ball, sentries stand pins back up, tentacles rise through open cracks.

Before each ball: What to knock down and what to leave standing: a pin on a crack holds it shut and adds Mult, so clearing the rack can be the wrong ball.

Lab: Medium: tentacles are the existing posts, cracks are a new rule; tuning is risky.

Risk: Its core verb, driving a pin into a tentacle, measured as dice: one pixel of aim moves where a pin ends up by half a pin spacing.

Physics-toy designer · critic #5

Pocket Deck

The back pit becomes five cups under a moving lamp. Sink pins in the lit cup; knocked pins that miss stay on the deck.

Before each ball: Which pins can reach the lit cup now, and what this ball leaves for the next cup. Measured: a non-strike wins the frame.

Lab: Medium: fallen pins have to persist on the deck.

Risk: It pays on where pins end up, the least stable thing measured. Banking pins may read as “balls behaving strangely”.

Swipe for all five. Ordered by the critic's rank: #1 is the one it thinks most likely to work.

Sides, yes. Exact pins, no.

Four of five pitches need you to choose which pins fall. The critic tested that in the Ten-Pin sim over 2,388 lines.

  • Aimable: keep the headpin up while 4 or more fall (window about 10 units), take one corner pin alone (about 7).
  • Not aimable: leave exactly the 7-10 (2.5 units at best), knock the headpin alone (no line), send pin 5 to a spot (one pixel moves it half a pin spacing).
  • Lane wear checks out: your usual line leaves the 9 after 2 units of drift and the 6-9 after 3.

Alarm-Pin Heist Wildcard

Each level is a job: one rack that stays put for four balls, where gold-collared loot pins pay and any ball that drops a red-collared alarm pin pays nothing.

The decision before each ball

  • Take or open. A clean ball pays its loot. A ball that drops an alarm pays $0, but the alarm is gone for good, so a deliberate $0 can open the pocket. Pins don't reset, so every ball picks your next leave.
  • Walk or throw. After the quota you may walk; unused balls pay $2 each. An alarm after the quota costs your whole surplus (shop money).
  • The guard. A dark pin too heavy to fall steps toward your last line, so repeating a line runs into him.

Worked example (quota $10, four balls)

1 = alarm, 3 = guard, 7 and 10 = $4, 5 = $3, 4 and 6 = $2, 2, 8 and 9 = $1. Outcomes are my estimates, not sim runs. - The usual strike line (start 0, aim +13, bend −90) drops the alarm, so it pays $0 and leaves about 7 and 10 ($8), short of the $10 quota. The job fails. - Left edge (start −60, aim −60): $7 from 4, 7 and 8, but the guard steps in front of the headpin and the alarm is still standing. - Thin the headpin from the right (start +40, aim +8): $0, but the alarm is gone. Ball 2 into the 4-5 gap pays $11 with two balls left: walk for $15, or throw for 6 and 10 ($6) and risk the surplus.

What you buy

  1. Muffler: the first alarm each job doesn't void its ball. Changes the best throw.
  2. Crowbar ball: drops the guard ($5), but pins scatter into alarms. Trade-off.
  3. Feather ball: little scatter, so edge shots are safe, but deep pins stand. Trade-off.
  4. Fence: loot that ends in a gutter pays double. Changes the best throw.
  5. Wire cutters: once per job, lift one alarm between balls. Changes shot order.
  6. Plant: before ball 1, add a plain pin on one of two spots. Changes the line.
  7. Getaway car: unused balls pay $4. Changes when you walk.
  8. Bigger bag: surplus ×1.5. Multiplier only.

How a boss works

The boss's floor plan shows on the level card from the start of the set. - Vault Door: the front two rows are all alarms and the loot is in the back row. Buy Wire cutters, the Feather ball or the Muffler, or spend two balls blowing the door. - Night Watchman: a guard on a fixed track (footprints show his next two steps) who parks in front of the richest loot. Buy the Crowbar ball: he pays $15 if he falls.

What it looks like

Same lane and camera, After Close look. Cream pins with collars: gold bands for loot value, red with a lamp for an alarm, and a dark body for the guard. Ball pips and a quota bar sit above the lane, with WALK beside READY. Shop pieces are big tools on a felt roll. Later racks add alarms and guards.

Failures broken

  • 2, board resets: the rack persists for four balls and the guard moves.
  • 3, one outcome: a strike through an alarm pays $0.
  • 4, builds multiply: most pieces change which ball is best.
  • 5, bosses as obstacles: you see the floor plan early and buy counters for it.
  • 6, balls as alternatives: the Crowbar and Feather balls are trade-offs.

Smallest lab

On labs/ten-pin/, throw unchanged: six jobs of four balls, with persistent pins, loot and alarm collars, a quota bar and WALK. No guard, no shop. Per ball, it logs the line, pins dropped by kind, whether an alarm fell, $ paid, walks and setup seconds. Kill it if one line takes over a third of balls, Josh never sacrifices or walks, or median setup stays near 2 seconds.

The risk

Alarms fall to pin scatter you can't read in advance. If an alarm dropping feels like a coin flip, avoiding them is frustration, not planning. The gaps between pins (about 9 units of clearance) may also be too tight to aim through.

Holdover Rack Systems designer

Pins standing at a frame's end stay put and gain value, and only the gaps refill from a bag of pins you build, so every ball weighs cashing pins now against ripening them for later, and bosses are games you ripen a crop for.

The decision before each ball

Which pins to fell and which to keep. Rules (ten-pin, two balls, five frames): - Plain pins are worth 1, +1 per frame standing (cap 5), shown as gold bands. - Fallen pins return to the bag; gaps refill from it at random. A crop carries into the level's next game. - A ball scores what it fells × Mult; no strike or spare bonus. Ball 2 can be passed (+$1).

Plain ripening is break-even on purpose (a pin left three frames pays 4, the same as felling a fresh pin there each frame); pieces tip it, and builds split there.

Worked example

Frame 3, ball 1. Build: Scythe (+1 Mult per ripe pin, worth 3 or more, the ball fells). Board: 7-pin worth 3, 9-pin worth 2, Keg on the 10 worth 5 (pays only to a direct ball hit), seven fresh pins.

Throw This frame Frame 4 Total
Pocket strike 12 (the Keg falls to a pin and pays 0) ×2 = 24 fresh rack, 10 34
Keg first, straight down the right board, then clean up 12 + 22 = 34 fresh rack, 10 44
Flush the headpin, leave the 7-10, pass ball 2 9 +$1 Keg (now 7) ×2 = 18, then clear with the 7-pin at 4: 20 47

The strike is worst. One more Keg or a Brace reorders them.

What you buy

Pins go into the bag tray, drawn big with their bands. ★ = changes what the best throw is. 1. Keg ★: ripens +2 a frame and pays only to a direct ball hit. 2. Brace ★: the pins it touches can't be knocked over by other pins. Shields crops, blocks strikes. 3. Fuse ★: fells its neighbours when it falls.; harvest or crop-wrecker. 4. Seed: its spot refills already worth 3. 5. Toll: −$1 each frame it stands, +$4 when it falls. 6. Scythe (gear): +1 Mult per ripe pin felled. The harvest engine. 7. Tip jar (gear): $1 per four fresh pins felled. Turnover engine, against Scythe. 8. Cellar (gear): a crop carried between games ripens +2. 9. Light ball ★ (little scatter: leaves crops cleanly, rarely strikes) or Heavy ball ★ (drives through: harvests, ruins crops).

How a boss works

Shown on the leave button from the level's start, so three shops can prepare. - Frost Lane: nothing ripens this game, and the target assumes you bring a crop. You end the big game with ripe pins standing yet still meet its target. Buy Brace, Cellar, Kegs. - Blight: standing pins lose 1 a frame. It punishes the ripening build that won the level before. Hedge with a Tip jar, Fuse, Seeds or a Heavy ball, or sell the Scythe.

What it looks like

Same lane and camera. Ripe pins wear gold bands; Keg squat and hooped, Brace iron-collared, Fuse black-headed. The bag is a row of pins up top, next draws visible. Over a level the rack turns gold.

Failures broken

  • 2: pin values come from your last ball; the bag moves Kegs, Braces, Fuses.
  • 3: a strike sells the crop early; with Scythe a leave outscores it.
  • 4: the ★ pieces change which pins you aim at or avoid.
  • 5: named a level ahead; they test a crop built across games.
  • 6: Light and Heavy balls trade crop-keeping against striking.

Smallest lab

On labs/ten-pin/, one game, throw unchanged, no shop. One mechanic: holdover and ripening with plain pins, Scythe built in (Mult = 1 + ripe pins felled), and a Pass button. - Bot check first: always-strike against best ripening policy, perfect release; within 10% of each other, it fails before Josh plays. - Log per ball: line, setup seconds, pin values before, pins felled, ripe felled, Mult, points, passed. - Kill: over two runs, his first-ball line repeats (within 5 units) on more than half the frames, or fewer than one frame in four ends with a ripe pin he chose to keep.

The risk

That the crop happens to him rather than being chosen: ten-pin scatter decides the leave, ripening is just a bonus on whatever survived, and he keeps throwing the strike line.

Lane Breakdown Bowling designer

Every ball dries the boards it rolls on and the lane isn't re-oiled until the next level, so strike lines drift and die; before each ball you choose where to spend fresh oil and which ball to spend it with.

The decision before each ball

  • Chase, move or save: pull the ring in on a worn track, open a fresh track, or throw something that burns nothing.
  • Which ball: strike ball (burns) or plastic spare ball (no burn, almost never strikes).
  • Small, big and boss share one oil, so the boss is bowled on the lane you leave.

Rule (to tune by bot): each pass on a track pushes the next ball on it 1 unit further along its hook. After six passes the track is dry wood: +3 a pass, carry fades.

Worked example

Level 1, big game, frame 4, Cranker next. Josh's line (start 0, ring +13, bend −90) has 5 passes; the right track (start +60, ring −12, bend +40) is fresh. Leaves are from the ten-pin sim with drift added; strike 20, spare ball pays pins × 2.

Throw Now Sets up
Same ring +13 6-9 leave, ~12 Track hits dry wood
Chase, ring +8 Strike, 20 Old line used up
Right track Strike, 20 Spends the track the Cranker won't touch
Plastic, start −20, ring −6 Leaves 10; cross-lane (−80, +40) converts: 11 Burns nothing

What you buy

  1. Sanded ball: wider pocket, burns 2 a pass. Changes the best throw.
  2. Polished ball: burns half; drifts double on a worn track. Changes.
  3. Plastic spare ball: bend locks straight, no burn, pins need a firmer hit (sim: straight strike lines 82 → 10 of ~5,000; 6-9 conversions 183 → 178). Changes.
  4. Bag strap: carry a second ball, pick before each throw.
  5. Oil stick: once a game, re-oil one track. Changes.
  6. Lane machine: re-oil everything before the boss. Expensive. Changes.
  7. Fresh Boards: +1 Mult on an untouched track; pays you to move, burns the lane faster. Changes.
  8. Pick-up pin: +2 Mult when it's the last pin standing and you convert it; a planned plastic 9-count becomes worth throwing. Changes.

How a boss works

  • The Cranker: a rival bowls your pair between frames, his big hook burning the centre 2 a pass; his total is the target. His track wears too, so he splits late, and you can burn it for him. The shop's boss card draws his line on your lane. Prepare: save the right track, buy Polished or an Oil stick.
  • Dry Lane: every track starts at 3 passes. The boss card shows a matte lane. Prepare: Polished, Plastic, Lane machine.

What it looks like

Gloss on the front two-thirds is oil. Each ball leaves a darker matte track along its real path, deeper each pass, with tick marks counting passes. The next level's re-oil sweeps it clean with the hall change. Shop balls drawn big: sanded scratched, polished highlighted, plastic translucent.

Failures broken

  1. Deterministic: the lane changes every ball; Josh's line leaves the 9 after two passes, the 6-9 after three.
  2. Board resets: the lane carries over three games.
  3. Bosses: seen in the shop, they wear your lane, and you prepare by saving tracks and buying surfaces.
  4. Balls: surfaces trade pocket width for lane life; plastic trades strikes for no burn.
  5. (partly) Oil stick, Lane machine and Pick-up pin change the best throw, not its size.

Smallest lab

Lane Breakdown Lab on labs/ten-pin/: one level on one oil, house ball, no shop, tracks drawn, throw unchanged (drift shifts where the ball arrives). Bot first counts how many distinct tracks strike on fresh oil. Log: track, passes, drift, start, ring, bend, leave. Kill if first balls stay 80%+ strikes from two or fewer start positions across three games (chasing became arithmetic), or Josh says the ring lies.

The risk

Drift is under a pixel on the lane camera, so it's read from the leave (9 = a touch high, 6-9 = high), as real bowlers do. If Josh reads leaves as random rather than as the lane telling him to move, this is Changing Field again.

Hold the Lane Art director and worldbuilder

Idea: ten-pin turns into a defence of the lane on a board that never resets, where a pin left standing can be worth more than one knocked down.

The decision before each ball

What to kill, what to leave standing, what moves next. - Pin (1 pt): standing on a crack, holds it shut. - Walker (2): steps one row toward you after each ball. Past the arrows it breaches: you lose your next ball. - Sentry (3): falls only to the ball. After each ball it stands its tethered pins back up. - Tentacle (4): rises from an open crack. Stops a ball dead; retracts when a pin hits it. - Points × Mult; Mult +1 per held crack at frame end. Each frame a new rank (top-left strip) enters at the back.

Worked example (designed board, not simulated)

Mult 2. Head pin on a crack; pins 3, 5, 6, 9; sentry on 10 tethered to 6 and 9; tentacle on 8; a walker one step short of the arrows. - Old pocket line: 9 × 2 = 18, but the walker breaches (ball 2 lost), a tentacle rises on the head spot, no Mult. - Walker, then the back door (the picture): 4, then the 5 into the tentacle and on into the sentry, 16. 20, crack held, Mult 3 after. - Through the walker into the pocket: about 24, but Mult stays 2 and a tentacle blocks the centre. Over two more frames the back door wins by about 30.

What you buy

  1. Lead-foot pin: falls, but keeps its crack shut. Changes the throw.
  2. Lure pin: walkers step toward it. Changes the throw.
  3. Kindling pin: whatever it touches falls, sentries and tentacles too. Changes the throw.
  4. Grapple ball: tears out a tentacle (4) instead of stopping; pins carry less. Changes the throw.
  5. Iron ball: pins it drives fell sentries; cracks it crosses open. Changes the throw.
  6. Floor nails (gear): tap one crack to nail it for the game. Changes the throw.
  7. Pit lamp (gear): two ranks ahead, cracks a ball early. Planning.
  8. Sandbag: held cracks give +2 Mult. Multiplier only.

How a boss works

  • The Root (game 3). In games 1 and 2 a dark grain line grows down one board, so you know its side. In game 3 a tentacle rises one row nearer along it each ball; a pin into the first kills it. Buy Kindling, Grapple, Lead-foot, Lures.
  • The Underside (game 12), after the ceiling cracks in game 10. Its shadow on the far lane (next ball's drawn faintly) lifts whatever stands in it, for nothing. Three cords fall only to pins. Buy Pit lamp, Lure, Kindling.

What it looks like

  • Silhouette first. Sentry: a pin gone dark iron, bolted foot, one lit slit, dotted tethers. Walker: a plate on three thin legs. Crack: a dark split, cold light inside. Tentacle: tall, banded, no face.
  • Over the run. Game 1 plain ten-pin; game 2 the pinsetter sets sentries; game 3 the boards split. After Close: lights out, invaders lit by their own slits and cracks. Computer: the scoring computer takes your side, drawing walker steps as phosphor ghosts; what it can't parse flickers violet. Arrival: chartreuse is the enemy's alone.
  • Shop. Each piece big on its own card, level as neck bands, drawn with what it acts on.

Failures broken

  • 2, board resets: it persists and moves.
  • 3, one outcome: clearing opens cracks and loses Mult; plugs pay.
  • 4, builds multiply: Lure, Kindling, Lead-foot and Nails move the best line.
  • 5, bosses: they show two games ahead; the shop sells counters.
  • 6, balls: Grapple and Iron trade one kind of piece against another.

Smallest lab

labs/ten-pin/, one five-frame game, throw unchanged, cracks and tentacles only. Open cracks raise tentacles (the boss post's stop; retract to a pin, 4 pts); held cracks +1 Mult; each frame adds three back pins and a crack. Logs: line, release, setup time, pieces down by kind, cracks held, distance from last line, score. Kill it if first balls mostly repeat one line, clearing tops the score nearly every frame, or tentacle hits feel like luck.

The risk

That a pin can be aimed. If sim scatter is too chaotic, "drive the 5 into the tentacle" is dice, not skill.

Pocket Deck Physics-toy designer

Idea: the back pit becomes five cups under a moving lamp, and knocked pins that miss the cups stay on the deck. You score by sinking pins in the lit cup, so every ball is a billiards shot on the board your last ball left.

Rules: a knocked pin pays 1. A sunk pin pays 1, or 5 in the lit cup. The lamp steps one cup after each ball, and the next cup glows dim. A frame is always two balls. A new rack stands pins only on clear spots. Strikes give no Mult.

The decision before each ball

Which pins can reach the lit cup now, and what this ball leaves for the next lamp.

Worked example (measured)

Measured in the Ten-Pin sim with cups added: 3,381 lines, perfect release. Lines are start / aim / bend. Full rack, lamp on cup 1, cup 2 next. - Strike line (0 / +10 / −90, Josh's usual): strike, 2 pins in the lit cup, 24. Leaves 4 loose pins, and the best ball 2 makes 10. Frame 34. - Right-side hook (+100 / +10 / +60): 8 down, 3 pins in the lit cup, 25. Leaves pins 3 and 10 standing and 3 loose. Ball 2 (−100 / 0 / −90) sinks 2 in cup 2, 14. Frame 39. - Rake (−100 / −20 / −30): a strike that sinks only 3, 17. Leaves 7 loose pins, and ball 2 sinks 3 in cup 2, 16. Frame 33.

The non-strike wins, and ball 2's best line is different after each ball 1.

What you buy

Mostly fixtures for the deck. Posts already exist in the sim; pin mass and wall bounce are constants. 1. Bumper post: pins bank off it, and it stops the ball dead. Changes the line. 2. Kick rail: one wall returns pins at full speed. Changes the line. 3. Lead pin (3× mass): ploughs through loose pins. Changes the line. 4. Cork pin (half mass): flies far, easy to sink. Changes the line. 5. Funnel cup: pulls in pins sliding nearby. Changes the line. 6. Second lamp: runs the opposite way. Changes the line. 7. Lamp holder: the lamp stays put after 2 or more pins land in it. Changes the plan. 8. Rake: loose pins slide 40 units toward the cups between frames. Changes the plan. 9. Chalk: a pin sunk off another loose pin pays double. Mostly multiplies. 10. Two-ball bag: Heavy breaks racks, Light caroms. You choose one each ball.

How a boss works

The level card shows the boss's deck one level ahead. - Shuttered cups: the middle three cups are closed. Counter with a kick rail, cork pins, or an outside funnel. - Tilted deck: pins drift left, and loose pins creep left between balls. Counter with a low-side post, lead pins, or a rake.

What it looks like

Same lane and camera. The back cushion becomes five dark cups, each with a brass lamp: one lit, the next dim. Loose pins lie where they stopped. Fixtures are brass and rubber, as on a bar-billiards table. The shop draws them big. The lead pin is dark grey and the cork pin tan, both with the red neck. Over a run your fixtures fill the deck.

Failures broken

  • 2, the board resets: loose pins stay, racks fill only clear spots, and the lamp moves.
  • 3, one outcome: pins pay by where they go, and a non-strike can win.
  • 4, builds multiply: fixtures and pin weights change the geometry.
  • 5, bosses: you see the deck a level ahead and buy counters.
  • 6, balls: Heavy against Light is a choice made each ball.
  • Not broken: 1. The release is still exact, but a repeated line meets a different board.

Smallest lab

labs/pocket-deck/, copied from labs/ten-pin/ with the throw unchanged. One game of 5 frames: cups, lamp and loose pins, no shop. - Log: line, lamp cup, sinks per cup, pins left, setup seconds, and the distance from the previous line. - Kill it if one line takes more than 3 of 10 balls, median setup stays under 3 s, strike-line frames score best, or sinking feels like luck.

The risk

Players may not be able to read where a loose pin will go from this camera without a preview. A ±1 change in aim moved ball 2 from 14 to 10. If cause and effect can't be felt, the lamp is a slot machine.
