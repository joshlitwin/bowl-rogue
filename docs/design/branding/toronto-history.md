# Toronto bowling history, for round 3 names (22 November 2026)

Research for the "Round 3: Toronto alleys" names. Paraphrased from the sources linked. **Unconfirmed** marks things only one source says, or where sources disagree. Some pages (Wikipedia's Thomas F. Ryan article, Bowl Canada's Hall of Fame page, a 5-pin history blog) couldn't be opened from this session, so only their search-result titles were seen. Their contents are not relied on here.

## Five-pin was invented in Toronto
- Thomas F. "Tommy" Ryan devised five-pin bowling around 1909 at his **Toronto Bowling Club**, after customers complained that the ten-pin game was too heavy and tiring. He cut ten-pins down to about three-quarters size and used a hand-sized hard rubber ball ([Wikipedia: Five-pin bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-pin_bowling); [Guelph Sports Hall of Fame](https://www.guelphsportshalloffame.com/builders/thomas-f-tommy-ryan)).
- The Canadian Encyclopedia gives **1908 or 1909** and says Ryan owned a ten-pin club in downtown Toronto. It also says he added a rubber band around each pin in **1912** to cut down on flying pins and noise ([Canadian Encyclopedia: indoor bowling](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/indoor-bowling)).
- In **1905** Ryan built what the Guelph Sports Hall of Fame calls Canada's first regulation ten-pin lanes, at the Toronto Bowling Club. It describes the club as a gentlemen-only Toronto hotspot whose regulars included John Eaton and Mayor Sam McBride ([Guelph SHOF](https://www.guelphsportshalloffame.com/builders/thomas-f-tommy-ryan)).
- **Where it was:**
  - One account puts Ryan's first regulation ten-pin alley **above a store at Yonge and Temperance Street**, citing the Ottawa Youth Bowling Club, and calls the club elite and private, for the carriage trade ([Glimpses of Canadian History](https://glimpsesofcanadianhistory.ca/?p=823)).
  - Metro puts Ryan's alley **near Yonge and Richmond** ([Metro](https://www.metro.us/living-history-of-5-pin-bowling/)).
  - Temperance Street runs west off Yonge one block south of Richmond, so the two descriptions fit the same corner. *Unconfirmed:* no source gives a street number, and the Glimpses piece doesn't explicitly say the Yonge-and-Temperance alley was the Toronto Bowling Club.
- Metro also says Ryan added the rubber rings after pins bounced out his window into the street ([Metro](https://www.metro.us/living-history-of-5-pin-bowling/)). *Unconfirmed* (one source).
- **1910:** the first five-pin league met at the Toronto Bowling Club ([Glimpses](https://glimpsesofcanadianhistory.ca/?p=823)).
- Ryan never patented the game and never profited from it. Glimpses says he was born in Guelph in 1872 and died in Toronto on 19 November 1961, and was inducted into the Canadian Sports Hall of Fame (1971) and the Bowl Canada Hall of Fame (2019) ([Glimpses](https://glimpsesofcanadianhistory.ca/?p=823); [Guelph SHOF](https://www.guelphsportshalloffame.com/builders/thomas-f-tommy-ryan)).
- A Toronto Star photo captioned "Tom Ryan, 5-pin bowling inventor" is in the Toronto Public Library digital archive ([TPL](https://digitalarchive.tpl.ca/objects/371741/tom-ryan-5pin-bowling-inventor)).

## Associations
- **1927:** about 500 bowlers met at Toronto's **King Edward Hotel** to found the Canadian Bowling Association ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/indoor-bowling)).

## Early clubs and lanes (photographs)
- **Queen City Bowling Club**, Church Street, about 1906: a photo of a three-storey brick building. The record doesn't say whether it was lawn bowling or alleys ([TPL](https://digitalarchive.tpl.ca/objects/386207/queen-city-bowling-club-toronto-ontario)). *Unconfirmed* as an alley. "Queen City" is an old nickname for Toronto.
- **Knights of Columbus Club** bowling alleys, Sherbourne Street, about 1910: a photo of the lanes ([Digital Archive Ontario](https://digitalarchiveontario.ca/objects/382341/bowling-alleys-knights-of-columbus-club-toronto-canada)).

## Halls that closed
- **Home Bowling Club**, on the second floor of a warehouse at **Queen Street East and Parliament**. Opened in 1925 or 1926 and ran until 1978. Its old entrance was uncovered when the building's exterior was restored in 2017 ([blogTO, 2017](https://www.blogto.com/city/2017/07/toronto-construction-revealed-old-bowling-alley/)).
- **Bayview Bowl**, on the west side of **Bayview Avenue between Davisville and Millwood**. Grand opening 5 November 1948, with 36 alleys of five- and ten-pin. Automatic pinsetters came in 1959. The owner announced closing in summer 1966, and a fire gutted the building on New Year's Day 1967 ([Leaside Life](https://leasidelife.com/?p=18946)). The same piece mentions the Leaside Bowling Centre (Eglinton East near Laird, late 1940s) and Thorncliffe Bowl (Thorncliffe Marketplace, early 1960s).
- **O'Connor Bowl** (O'Connor Drive, East York): lasted 65 years and was demolished for condos ([blogTO, 2017](https://www.blogto.com/sports_play/2017/03/bowling-alleys-toronto/)). The 2015 closing article couldn't be opened; its headline says it closed just short of its 65th birthday ([blogTO, 2015](https://www.BlogTo.com/sports_play/2015/06/oconnor_bowl_to_close_just_shy_of_its_65th_birthday/)). That puts the opening around 1950. *Unconfirmed:* exact address and opening year.
- **Bathurst Bowlerama**, 2788 Bathurst Street at Glencairn. Opened 1962, closed 15 December 2018 for a condo and hotel project ([blogTO, 2018](https://www.blogto.com/sports_play/2018/12/bathurst-bowlerama-closing-toronto-after-56-years/); [CTV](https://toronto.ctvnews.ca/bowlers-take-to-the-lanes-for-final-time-before-bathurst-bowlerama-knocked-down-1.4219969)).
- **Bowlerama West**, Dundas West: closed 31 January (2018) for condos. The Toronto Historical Bowling Society held a farewell ([blogTO, 2017](https://www.blogto.com/city/2017/12/toronto-losing-another-bowling-alley-condos/)).
- **Thorncliffe Bowlerama**: closed for a fitness centre ([blogTO, 2017](https://www.blogto.com/sports_play/2017/03/bowling-alleys-toronto/)).

## Still operating, or may be (avoid as names)
- **Shamrock Bowl**, 280 Coxwell Avenue at Gerrard: a 1950s five-pin alley, restored in 2009 and closed in 2011. It was set to reopen as Shamrock Bowl and Restobar in February 2019 ([Beach Metro, 2017](https://beachmetro.com/2017/08/23/shuttered-retro-bowling-alley-looks-pin-new-tenant/); [blogTO, 2019](https://media.blogto.com/city/2019/02/new-bowling-alley-toronto)). Treat it as live.
- **Bowlerama** (e.g. Rexdale Bowlerama, 115 Rexdale Blvd, home of a league since 1983, [IGBO](https://www.igbo.org/?p=33373)), **Danforth Bowl**, **Kennedy Bowl**, **West Bowlerama** and **The Ballroom** (John Street, 2010) were all operating per blogTO in 2017 ([blogTO](https://www.blogto.com/sports_play/2017/03/bowling-alleys-toronto/)). Not checked for 2026.
- **King Edward Hotel**: a live hotel brand, so not used.
- Playdium and "Bowl-O-Rama" (named in the brief) were not searched by name; a general search with "Bowl-O-Rama" in it brought up only the Bowlerama closings. Not used.
